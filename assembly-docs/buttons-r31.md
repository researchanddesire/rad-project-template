# R31 button module target sequence

Use the current native R31D file and its matching review records. This is an
unperformed prototype sequence. The 0.58 mm displayed stop is a CAD study;
establish an operating setting from switch/coupon results before powered use.
Keep the battery and external charging supply disconnected during mechanical
assembly and continuity calibration.

1. Make the cover, bezel and two insulating plungers to the native nominal
   geometry. Ream/finish the Ø2 mm guides and tap the M2/M1.6 pilots using
   actual received parts. Inspect the thin SW2 guide/stop walls, countersink
   finish, edge radii and support lands; reject cracks, burrs or interference.
2. Match the integral cradle lands to the received flange PCB's bare edge
   bands. Establish the board position and restraint before button calibration.
   Check actual rear components and board deflection under the intended force.
   Do not use the switch or its solder joints as an assembly stop.
3. At the bench, feed each plunger tip-first through its cover guide. Fit its
   transverse Ø0.8×3 mm home pin through the nominal Ø0.78 mm bore. Inspect
   retention and free sliding after fitting; the CAD interference is a process
   candidate, not proof that the pin stays captive. The pin bears on the front
   guide face at rest. Account for both pins before closing the device.
4. Fit two M2×4 stop candidates. The **socket-end face** faces the plunger
   arm; it must present an inspected flat annulus at least Ø1.9 mm outside,
   around the Ø1.4 mm arm access hole. A stock chamfered set screw needs this
   inspection; a custom flat-back stop is an alternative. Check actual thread
   engagement across the adjustment range and the 0.9 mm drive/key fit.
5. Seat the silicone head cups over the plunger heads and the common lid rim
   in the cover recess. Fit the bezel with two M1.6×5 countersunk candidates.
   Inspect actual compression: the free-rim study is 0.6 mm and installed rim
   0.5 mm. Check flush screw heads, diaphragm freedom, head capture and full
   return to the home pins. Material, hardness, cure and torque remain process
   trials; no seal or return-force qualification is transferred from CAD.
6. Assemble the body and hubs using the R30 loose, unstripped/unhoused flange
   wire ends. Slide the populated flange board into the new locating lands.
   Form the current R31 loops, trim and terminate to the fixture and current
   ten-net map, then make/cure the flange anchor externally. The R29 recipe's
   120 mm blanks remain starting stock, not finished cut lengths. Use the R31
   plan for the revised rise/loops. Keep the rear module and flange skins off
   while fitting the existing one-screw keeper.
7. Offer the complete button-cover module from the rear. The finite CAD poses
   have the flange skins waiting. Check each wire against the guide and stop
   bosses, keeper and board. Verify free gap at both switches and no preload
   with the board in its final restraint. Do not force a misaligned tip.
8. With the silicone lid/bezel removed for access, use a straight Ø1.3 mm
   shaft allowance and the actual suitable key through each Ø1.4 mm arm hole
   to adjust its stop. Refit the lid and repeat the measurement; its assembled
   influence is part of calibration. The shaft check is not an actual drive
   engagement or hand-access test.

## Commissioning the controls

First obtain controlled allowable travel/load data or use sacrificial coupons
with a force/displacement fixture. Measure actual electrical make/release,
switch compression, boot return and stop contact; inspect for damage and test
cycling at the real circuit's contact voltage/current. Do not treat catalog
pretravel as allowable overtravel or assume a published static force is a
repetitive operating load.

For each matched assembly, establish a stop inside that demonstrated range,
with no switch preload at rest and repeatable switching before the rigid stop.
Record both settings, force/displacement results and pin/board positions.
Qualify a locking method compatible with the materials; mark the stop positions
and check drift after vibration and repeated presses. Verify power, mode,
return and accidental-press behavior on the assembled cover. If any requirement
cannot be met, revise the button design before powered enclosed operation.

Check diaphragm/perimeter sealing using an unpowered leak-test article before
exposing electronics to cleaning or liquid. Inspect all exposed seams, fastener
recesses and cleaning access. Passing a bench control or leakage experiment
does not establish finished-surface, contact-duration or wear acceptance.

The current native geometry and detailed limits are described in
`developer-docs/docs/buttons-r31.md`. Battery selection and the human-owned
product BOM remain unchanged. Remaining harnesses, pack/NTC retention, flange
loads and final seals still need a complete prototype build proposal.
