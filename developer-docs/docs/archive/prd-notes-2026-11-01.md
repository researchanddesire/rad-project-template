# Product Requirements

Working draft, updated 1 November 2026. These requirements come from Lucy's current notes and supersede the previous specification. Lucy and Gaby have started the schematic.

## Required features

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance to define or verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRD-01 | WiFi | Agree connection behavior, commands and radio duty cycle. |
| PRD-02 | Bluetooth | Draft controller supports Bluetooth LE; confirm the intended Bluetooth interface. |
| PRD-03 | On/off switch | Actuate through local silicone at the polymer flange; define its mounting/sealing and verify reliable switching. |
| PRD-04 | Mode switch | Actuate through local silicone at the polymer flange; define modes, press behavior and sealing. |
| PRD-05 | Two-pin bullet vibration motor breakout | Confirm motor part, polarity, connector and wire routing. |
| PRD-06 | PWM motor control | Define range and modes, then verify driver current and motor behavior. |
| PRD-07 | Lithium battery charging circuit | Confirm cell chemistry, charging limits, supply and charge behavior. |
| PRD-08 | Lithium battery discharge protection | Select and verify protection against the agreed battery fault conditions. |
| PRD-09 | Internal battery connector | Confirm connector, battery envelope and retention. |
| PRD-10 | Wired temperature sensor at the plug tip | Place the sensing element at the tip; define accuracy, sampling and thermal response. |
| PRD-11 | Accelerometer | Define purpose, axes, sampling and host interface. |
| PRD-12 | Two magnetic charging contacts reaching the outer flange surface | Define isolated contact/magnet mounting in the polymer flange, sealing, polarity and charging supply. “MagSafe-style” describes the concept; no Apple compatibility is specified. |
| PRD-13 | Coccyx and perineal clearance | Define target anatomy and postures, then assess clearance, local contact and pressure distribution. No clearance dimension or pressure limit is established. |
| PRD-14 | Smooth, low-profile base following the pelvic contour | Compare editable outer contours for movement, sitting and access. Define surface, edge and comfort acceptance before choosing internal dimensions. |
| PRD-15 | Retained, accessible flared stop and removal feature | Define retention, deformation and removal checks; contouring must preserve an effective external stop and a grip that remains accessible. |
| PRD-16 | Body-to-base alignment adjustable during the contour study | Vary the relative angle and position as design parameters, then choose the alignment. No product adjustment mechanism is specified. |
| PRD-17 | Thermal review of every contact region | Define sensing coverage, normal/fault conditions, response and limits; assess the motor, battery and electronics hot spots separately from the tip reading. |
| PRD-18 | Material and finished-surface review for the intended contact | Define formulation, cure, pigments, seams, cleaning and lubricant compatibility, contact duration and appropriate evaluation. Material names alone do not establish acceptance. |

## Intended wear

The primary use is awake wear with removal breaks. A secondary design goal is continuous wear including sleep, requested for experienced users. Experience does not establish the safety of this mode. Wear duration, break conditions and sleep-use acceptance remain to define and validate; neither mode has been physically or clinically validated.

The 24-hour target below is battery endurance under an agreed electrical usage profile. It is separate from permitted wear duration and does not establish 24-hour continuous-wear safety.

The anatomy-first requirements are consistent with [Planned Parenthood Toronto's guidance](https://ppt.on.ca/factsheets/anal-play/) on suitable toy surfaces, flexibility and an accessible flared base. [Oxford University Hospitals NHS guidance](https://www.sexualhealthoxfordshire.nhs.uk/services/psychosexual-therapy/lubricants/) describes the anal and rectal lining as vulnerable to friction, sharp features and sudden movement. These sources do not establish a continuous-wear or sleep-use duration.

[ISO 3533:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/79631.html) addresses materials, design safety and user information for manufactured sexual products contacting the genitals or anus. Its public scope does not establish a wear-time allowance or product conformity. Review the applicable requirements as the design develops.

## Battery target

Ideally 24 hours when the motor is used lightly. This is a target, not a measured result. Before choosing capacity, agree the motor runtime and PWM profile, WiFi/Bluetooth active and sleep time, sensor sampling and usable cell capacity. Validate the agreed profile on a prototype.

Battery selection is explicitly open. The 350 mAh Bitcraze pack used in earlier
calculations and CAD is a dimensional/electrical reference, not a selected part.

## Packaging

**Latest assembly constraints, 1 November:** couple the vibration motor to the
metal body through its own mount. Support the PCB independently; the rejected
R21 shared motor/PCB keeper is historical evidence only. The
[R25 independent mount](motor-shell-mount-r25.md) develops a keyed metal
clamp seated on the body rim and captured by the cap. Its rigid nominal
checks pass; actual contact/preload, wires and physical retention remain open.
Flexible motor leads
connect the two assemblies. Define motor retention, contact/preload and the
vibration load path without using the PCB or its keeper as a motor support.

[R26F](tip-support-r26.md) now adds a detachable stationary tip support and
four individually routed wire allowances, preserving that independent motor
mount. Its native/source and listed assembly checks pass their scopes. The
clip/thermal bond/gel are process candidates; actual wire exits, terminations,
cure, installed thermal response and physical fit remain unqualified. Fit the
tip after the motor and keep free wire ends during loading; do not assume a
finished connected harness follows the large rigid CAD offsets.

Semi-flex PCB construction is allowed. A 90° rotation of the flange PCB is
also allowed if it improves packaging without making the flange too wide.
The outside contour leads the board layout; do not enlarge the flange just
to preserve a previous board orientation. Compare the complete populated
assembly, connector/wire space, antenna clearance and button access.
Distinguish a bend made during installation from the connection that flexes
during wear. See the [R24 orientation screen](flange-orientation-r24.md).

The [R27D cradle](flange-cradle-r27.md) develops the X90 option with unchanged
exterior. [R28S](flange-routing-r28.md) now repairs the two edge-facing header
routes while preserving the full circuit; both native boards have zero routing
findings. This does not implement semi-flex or working local controls. Real
supported wiring, flange compliance/removal loads and physical fit remain open.

**Current exploration: one-piece metal body, larger front-loading cap and
separate body/flange PCBs.** Lucy asked on 31 October to prioritize simpler
assembly and explore a flexible connection at the flange. The
[R13 drop-in study](simple-assembly-r13.md) develops this direction with actual
[R12 board placement](split-pcb-r12.md). Lucy clarified on 1 November that the
inward nose scoop is unnecessary: the new cap has a fuller rounded taper,
retaining the 15 mm neck, body diameter and length. The previous split shell, common male thread,
rear clamp bolt and single rigid T-board remain preserved development
baselines. They are no longer fixed architecture requirements. The cap still
supplies the external nose closure; the tip electronics remain stationary.
R14 now provides native two-board circuits and complete-device ERC.
The [R23D body](body-stackup-r23.md) now has zero native
DRC/unconnected/footprint findings on a provisional six-layer stack. The
flange remains the preserved routed four-layer board. J1 is an SH2 candidate and connector
mating directions change. Fresh R15 GUI ERC, actual supported wiring and
manufacturing/process review remain open; see [the R15 record](flange-routing-r15.md).
Flange retention/compliance, supported interconnect and sealing still
need design and validation.

Lucy rejected a 25 mm neck as too wide for the intended fit and selected
**15 mm** for the next shape study. Develop a smooth waist; this target is
not a wear-duration acceptance. Keep components out of the narrow neck and
develop the new body/flange board outlines and flexible link around the exterior. The
electrically checked R0 layout is a preserved baseline, not a fixed mechanical
envelope. Battery selection remains open.

Lucy wants a high-quality polished metal finish. The earlier split-shell
proposal called for polishing the matched empty assembly to conceal its seam;
the current one-piece direction removes that longitudinal seam. The proposed manufacturing sequence
uses an empty matched assembly for finishing, then cleaning and final
electronics assembly. Seam registration, sealing and retention need their own
features. Threadlocker is intended as a candidate process; product, application,
cure, compatibility and seal performance remain unselected.

Power and mode switches remain pressed through local elastomer at the flange;
cover, membrane and sealing details need development. The battery stays inside,
the wired sensing element stays at the metal tip and isolated charging contacts
reach the flange surface. Keep the tip PCB and wires stationary while the cap
rotates; define the dielectric thermal interface and seating stop separately.

The current circuit separates the main electronics into body and flange
islands with a compliant interconnect; these may use separate boards or a
supplier-reviewed semi-flex/rigid-flex construction. The existing remote tip island would be a
third rigid board unless integrated into the body assembly. X spans the
handle, Y points toward the tip and Z is normal to the board. The exterior
leads packaging. No rigid PCB, interconnect or elastomer cover alone is a
qualified flexible flange or removal-load path.

The supplied schematic's earlier ≤25 mm PCB diameter note is an unresolved reference, not a frozen envelope for the selected topology. Concept 02's board dimensions are provisional space reservations. Use [Concept 03: primary contour study](design-proposal-v3.md) to develop the anatomy-first outer shape before fixing the PCB outline. No contour, board or body fit is validated.

The [Plan A material and antenna study](materials-and-rf-study.md) records the
earlier metal-body/polymer-flange basis. The preserved R2 baseline adds the split body, cap, rear bolt
and rubber cover; the new one-piece study also preserves the nonmetal antenna region at the outward
flange end. No alloy, surface specification, wall thickness, polymer/elastomer
grade, joint seal or cleaning method is approved. This is not a return to a
full silicone-overmold body. Fit, sealing, radio performance and sensing
accuracy remain unvalidated.

## Motor reference

Lucy linked the [NFP-E1015 motor listing](https://nfpshop.com/product/nfp-e1015-encapsulated-vibration-motor-with-metal-cover-7g-vibration-force). The published reference is 3.7 V DC, 450 mA maximum rated current and 9,500 ±2,000 rpm. Its drawing shows approximately Ø11 ×26 mm. See the [manufacturer datasheet](https://nfpshop.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Encapsulated-Vibration-Motor-NFP-E1015-3.7V.pdf).

The notes also call the motor an “N20 bullet”. Confirm the actual part before sizing the driver, battery, connector or cavity. Rated current is not a validated startup or stall-current limit for this assembly.
